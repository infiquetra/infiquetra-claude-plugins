## Findings

### F1 — The gates caught real problems before they cost anything

The ideation surfaced that nothing machine-parses the schema fields (the consumer is an LLM + a human), which killed two heavyweight options before they were planned. Then doc-review grepped the templates and caught that the plan over-stated its remediation — only 1 of 9 templates actually had the collapse — preventing the parallel fan-out from over-editing eight clean files.

Operator confirmed at interview: the full chain was right-sized for this change.

### F2 — The ultracode fan-out needed a verification pass

The four parallel agents produced good edits but hedged on the link convention (clickable vs bare code-span, some emitting both). A post-fan-out normalization + diff review caught it, plus a hard-coded version-pin test that needed bumping. Promoted to LEARNINGS {#fanout-agents-hedge-conventions}.

### F3 — R5 (no-hard-wrap) is encoded but not retrofitted (deferred)

The contract prescribes no-hard-wrap for generated output, but the jumbling problem is already solved by blank-line separation + tables, so the earlier session docs' hard-wrapping is cosmetic. Operator chose "leave as-is, opportunistic." Queued as {#saga-doc-soft-wrap-r5}; this retro doc and the QA doc are written no-hard-wrap as the opportunistic demonstrations.
